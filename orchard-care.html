Late Winter Dormant Spray to Control Pests & Diseases

By Lawrence MartinMarch 26, 20246 min read

“When an ounce of prevention is truly worth a pound of cure.”

In this guide
The dormant spray kit Timing is crucial Mixing your spray Applying dormant spray Staying safe Pollinator friendly Peach leaf curl Mixing formulas

Many bugs and diseases overwinter on fruit trees and shrubs. Horticultural oil and liquid lime sulphur, combined, deliver a one-two knock-out punch to overwintering insects and diseases on fruit trees, shrubs and ornamentals.

Our Green Earth Dormant Spray Kit (SKU 735-6150) is a naturally based product and a great tool in the hands of organic gardeners who want to give their fruit trees a healthy start to the growing season — part of the going-green movement.

The Green Earth Dormant Spray Kit

The kit consists of two products that work together:

  • 1
    Horticultural oil

    Designed to kill overwintering insects, insect eggs and some caterpillars by coating and suffocating them.

  • 2
    Lime sulphur

    Particularly effective against fungal diseases — blister and rust mites, pear scale, red mite eggs, aphid eggs, twig borers, powdery mildew, apple scab, pear rust, plum black knot and peach leaf curl. Also great for black spot on roses.

Timing is crucial

Pay careful attention to timing and apply dormant oil and lime sulphur before bud break — before vegetative or flowering buds begin to sprout.

Pick the right day
Choose a dry, frost-free day when temperatures will stay above freezing for 24–48 hours. If possible, spray in the morning so trees have ample time to dry before nightfall. Avoid spraying on a windy day.

Mixing your spray

As with any product, take the time to carefully read the label and product guide, which explain how to use the product and the mixing ratios. Ratios vary according to the targeted plants, purpose and timing.

To use the Green Earth Dormant Spray Kit at this time of year, mix 120 ml (8 tbsp) of lime sulphur concentrate and 60 ml (4 tbsp) of horticultural oil in 3 litres of water. Full ratios for every crop are in the formulas below.

Applying dormant spray

Hand-pump pressure sprayers, battery-pack sprayers or hose-end sprayers can all be used (see Whiffletree’s line of sprayers, pg. 79–80). To be effective, spray your trees thoroughly — even to the point of dripping wet.

The horticultural oil must contact overwintering insects and eggs hiding in bark ridges, crevices and branch crotches; the oil covers their surface and suffocates them. When trees are dormant, the spray can be safely used on all your fruit trees.

Staying safe

The kit is easy to use and, as a naturally based product, is considered organic — but don’t underestimate its potency.

Follow all precautions
Wear chemical-resistant gloves, a long-sleeved shirt, long pants and eye protection, and avoid all skin contact (watch for wind and spray drift). Wash your face and hands after use. Mix only what you can use at once — once mixed with water, the spray doesn’t keep. Because lime sulphur stains painted wood, brick, stone and concrete, cover such items before spraying nearby.

Pollinator friendly

Applying these sprays before your fruit trees and shrubs break dormancy avoids harming honey bees, butterflies and other beneficial insects, which are not yet active.

Peach leaf curl

Some common fungal diseases can only be controlled preventively. Peach leaf curl is a very common fungal disease that can defoliate, weaken and eventually kill peach and nectarine trees. Since the fungus overwinters on the bark and in the bud scales, the optimal time to spray is in spring before leaf break — or, at the latest, just before bloom.

Both lime sulphur and copper are organic fungicides that control peach leaf curl. Anyone planting peach and nectarine trees should consider using one of these products. For more, see the OMAFRA guide to peach leaf curl.
